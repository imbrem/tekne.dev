# Dubai: First

My battery died on the plane, and I was _tired_. We were back in the desert.

Dubai is an interesting place. Much has changed, and much has stayed the same, as the truism goes.

We land. It's been a long time, and it's hot, for one. That's quite familiar, actually. I remember
when there was that 40ᵒC heatwave in Cambridge and everyone was panicking, and I just felt like I
was back home for a day.

Home. An interesting concept. I never fully understood it.

Anyways, I arrive at my uncle's house and send him a message on GoChat, a local WhatsApp clone. He
doesn't see it... turns out I used his old number. 

I'm _very_ tired. I take my contact lenses out, and my left eye is somewhat irritated. Which makes
sense considering I dropped the lens and rinsed it in regular water, pressed for time.

I didn't want to wake him, so I just slept on the pool chair outside. I mean, my judgement was
impaired by extreme sleep deprivation, and it was warm.

Turns out they stayed up, seeing if I would come. But I was outside.

So one thing you notice about the desert is that there's a lot of flies. One of the many reasons
you've got to take a lot of showers. Mosquitoes too. It interfered with my otherwise quite
comfortable position on the pool chair.

Compare this to field in Italy, where the issue was instead rocks and cold.

Anyways, I finally get inside, and can shower and change. Leave the lenses out for today, as my eye
is still irritated, though better. Still tired. Have breakfast and talk a bit, about to go outside,
and... pass out.

For a long time I leave this world. 

It's 4 PM, I'm back up, and somehow I need to get from here to a functioning presentation. But I've
got nothing to draw it with, as I forgot my graphics tablet at home, and I really don't want to futz
around with Typst.

So we need to get a graphics tablet. I also need to see an old friend. I thought I'd parallelize.

Now we have $2^2$ problems.

Some "electrolytes" later (it was pre-workout... he didn't change...)

The tablet remains staunchly unbought, the schedule irrecoverably shifted, and rather than shift it
further by taking an Uber back to my uncle's, I just crash on the couch. There's a weighted blanket,
which is nice. He really sold it.

I am awoken at 7 AM (he calls this a "lay in") to refreshingly tasteful music, which I try to ignore
for as long as possible. But it's not the worst way to be recalled to the world of the living. I am
given black instant coffee, which at first I recoil at, but eventually I learn to sip.

A bit more of the pre-workout helps, too.

He is off to face the jobsite, and I am off to face the dawn. From here to presentation. Which way
does HOPE lie? I'm sorry...

Anyways.

I began by looking around Dubai Hills for a graphics tablet, which I thought would be an easy task,
except no-one had a graphics tablet. Someone had one of those cheap fluorescent stablets with the
reset button, but that obviously would not do.

So I bite the bullet, get the cheapest iPad I can find, and, excuses exhausted, set my hands to
labor.

When we face what we fear, it is always simultaneously better and worse than what we imagined.

I alternate between hacking and meeting with Neel, and slowly, the thing takes shape. A few days
pass in this manner.

I take a break to pass by Green Community, where in elder days I grew up. Took a walk through the
east side. Felt like one of those cutscenes.

More presenting.

On the very last day, my friend's birthday, I am convinced to let him cut my hair. This was a
mistake of horrific proportions.

I go home to my uncle's to fetch my luggage, and, confronted with what can only be described as a
crime in the mirror, fall to weeping. I hope they've got good barbers to fix this mess in Singapore.

It's early the next morning when my consciousness is once more dragged into the world by a litany of
alarms, each reinforcing the last like the layers of some gambeson of wakefulness. Or something.

I arrive at the airport, and, tablet in hand, continue hammering my presentation into shape.

I doze briefly on the flight to Singapore, my talk mostly completed after the first half. I can
rarely get any sleep at all on a plane... I curl up and it hurts my tailbone.

We land at early evening, and, after buying a SIM and getting some cash, I take a taxi to the Hotel
Bencoolen Hong Kong Street, not to be confused with the Hotel Bencoolen on Bencoolen Street.

In front of the hotel is a long row of nightclubs, on one side of which can be found excellent food,
and on the other side of which, after some walking, is Chinatown, which is also replete with, you
guessed it, excellent food.

I sit down for a Vietnamese meal and practice my presentation, calling an old friend to subject her
to my first attempt at giving the thing in full.

I think I will like Singapore. But there remains much to repair. It is much too late when I briefly
return to the dream world, to draw precious few drops of strength.
